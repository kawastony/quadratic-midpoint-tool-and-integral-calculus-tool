# Theories that can use these dynamics

**Date:** 2026-10-09
**Status:** Separation. The identity is usable. The funnel schedule is an external constraint. The kink packet cannot use either as its dynamics.

## Quadratic identity

For f(x) = x^2 the midpoint slope equals the sum of the boundary coordinates and the average of the boundary slopes. That is exact.

It is the dynamics of a quadratic potential, classical or quantum. Force is linear, F = -f'. The force at the midpoint is the average of the endpoint forces. Ehrenfest's theorem carries the same statement onto expectation values in a harmonic oscillator: d<p>/dt = -m omega^2 <x>. A Gaussian wave packet in that well stays Gaussian. Symmetric collapse of the sample window does not move the midpoint.

Any smooth potential is locally quadratic. The identity is then a diagnostic of the midpoint force, as in version 4 note 31. It is not the global dynamics.

## Funnel schedule

Fixed arrival time T = integral dx / v(x), average speed L/T, pause frozen, active elongation. This is a prescribed schedule.

Classical continuity can use it. Closing speed u sets an axial speed. Acceleration is v dv/dx, not u. The integral ledger already says this, and already says the integrals do not show a constant g.

A quantum fluid can use the schedule only as an imposed trap. Gross-Pitaevskii or a Tonks gas in a waveguide whose length is driven between pauses inherits L(t) from outside. The arrival-time constraint is not a solution of the Schrödinger equation. Quantum arrival time is not fixed by a classical integral of 1/v.

A driven two-level or harmonic system can use the pause/active split as a control schedule: Hamiltonian frozen on pause, parameters moved on active. The impulse (L' - L)/T is then a kick between pauses. The kick is put in, not derived.

## Cannot use these as their dynamics

The kink packet. Its clock is T = pi / Delta E, not L/T. Its split is an energy, not a closing speed. Version 5 note 32.

Callias index, cone geodesics, and the regular Riemann tensor. None has a fluid, a closing wall, or an L^2 kinetic cost.

A theory that needs a derived universal g. This repo already records that the integrals do not show one.
