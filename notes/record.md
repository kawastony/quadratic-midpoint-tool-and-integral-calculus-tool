## 1. Quadratic midpoint tool

On an \(x^2\) graph, the derivative where \(x=3\) is \(6\). If the boundaries are \(2\) and \(4\), adding them gives \(6\). The derivative at \(2\) is \(4\) and the derivative at \(4\) is \(8\); their average is \(6\).

Starting from boundary points \(-1\) and \(7\) and pushing the boundary points together, the midpoint stays the same.

Identity used by that example, for \(f(x)=x^2\) and \(f'(x)=2x\):

\[
f'(m)=x_1+x_2=\frac{f'(x_1)+f'(x_2)}{2}=\frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1}.
\]

Any pair with the same sum has the same midpoint and the same midpoint slope. Symmetric collapse onto that midpoint does not change the slope reading. Only the interval shrinks.

## 2. Funnel, held exact

The cone’s volume is exact and unchanging. The circumference is also fixed. The cone, or its funnel shape, is stretched. The funnel can elongate infinitely. Elongation moves between an active phase and a pause phase.

Water always starts flowing from the circumference and goes all the way to the tip. It always reaches the end at the same time.

The speed at which the boundary points come together becomes the acceleration experienced within the cone by the water flowing through it.

Integral calculus, used to track that phenomenon in real time, is the proposed measure of energy depleted by the falling experience in the cone. That energy, or its rate, is proposed as a gravity measure. The pressure applied by the cone is the proposed correspondent of the acceleration.

A similar right circular cone has volume \(\tfrac13\pi r^2 h\). Fixed circumference fixes \(r\), which then fixes \(h\). Stretch with both invariants held is therefore not a similar cone. The circumference and the volume stay as fixed. The generators are what change.

## 3. Closing speed and acceleration

Let the boundaries approach the fixed midpoint at closing speed \(u\) (length per time). Water acceleration is length per time squared, so \(u\) does not become the acceleration as the same quantity.

With volume and circumference held fixed, liquid displaced by the closing wall is drawn along the stretch. Continuity sets an axial speed \(v\) from \(u\) and from the funnel at that instant. The water accelerates only if \(v\) changes: the closing speeds up, or the particle runs into a tighter section, \(a=v\,\mathrm dv/\mathrm dx\). Constant closing speed through a uniform section gives constant axial speed.

## 4. Same arrival time on an infinite elongation

Pause: length \(L\) frozen. Trip time

\[
T=\int_0^{L}\frac{\mathrm dx}{v(x)}.
\]

Same \(T\) on every longer pause forces average speed \(L/T\), which grows without bound as the funnel elongates. A launch speed matched to the current length arrives on time at constant velocity. Acceleration during the trip is required if every run leaves the circumference at the same initial speed, or if the water is flowing while the tip is still receding.

## 5. Integral ledger

Power exchanged with the liquid is pressure times wall speed, integrated over the wetted surface. In the ideal liquid that work reappears as kinetic energy of the axial extension, or as pressure. Depletion is the part removed by viscosity or turbulence.

Gravity is the height term. Gravitational power of liquid shifted by \(\Delta h\) is \(\dot m\, g\, \Delta h\). The hydrostatic part of wall pressure is \(\rho g h\). A horizontal stretch has wall work and no gravitational power. A vertical stretch has both. The integral measures gravity only in the part of the pressure that was already hydrostatic.
