# Assessment of Planck floor, catenary, Gamma, and midpoint diagnostic

Date: 18 October 2026

## Planck floor and midpoint theorem

Setting R(0) = ℓ_P is a choice of boundary condition, not a derivation. The constitution assigns the floor as ξ = GM/c² (mass-dependent; 1.48 km for the Sun). A universal Planck floor replaces that assignment and is incompatible with the existing closure except for a Planck-mass object. On the linear profile R(s) = R0 + λs the midpoint identity is exact but idle; the derivative is λ everywhere. Relative contribution of ℓ_P is ∼10^{-34} or smaller for laboratory or astrophysical lengths.

## Catenary

The profile R = a cosh(s/a) has constant negative Gaussian curvature K = -1/a². It forces a choice:
- Keep lapse N = s/(s+ξ) and catenary R: reciprocal closure broken, g_ss ≠ 1/N², light bending fails (γ ≠ 1).
- Keep closure and force catenary: Newtonian 1/s² regime disappears; force goes as 1/s then saturates at constant c²/a.
The free scale a is forced to the Hubble radius to match observed cosmic acceleration, so the profile is invisible in laboratory or black-hole regimes. It is a static equilibrium shape. No equation of motion is added. The proposal costs one of the two existing successes and does not address plasma data. Removal is correct.

## Gamma function

For fixed d = 3, Γ(5/2) = (3/4)√π is a pure number. It appears in ball-volume formulae or certain partition functions, neither of which is present in the cone grammar. It supplies no scaling law and does no work here.

## Curvature remainder diagnostic

The quadratic midpoint identity has a remainder controlled by the third derivative. On a linear profile the remainder is identically zero. On any curved profile it is not. Applied to data, a vanishing remainder confirms the linear cone; a non-zero remainder measures the curvature that is present. This is a free, falsifiable diagnostic. It does not require assuming the shape in advance.

## Route to dark energy / MOND

If data at large scales produce a non-zero curvature remainder well described by a hyperbolic profile with curvature radius near the Hubble scale, the framework would predict a residual acceleration of order c H0. This is conditional on the data. It is not obtained by choosing the catenary. The linear cone remains the working profile where the remainder is zero.

## Laws

No update required. The six laws already encode equal phases, a = field, the midpoint identity, shell transmission, one-end fixed circumference, and reciprocal closure. Light–geometry coupling and elongation under tip-speed saturation are consequences of those laws plus the speed bound. Catenary and Gamma are external choices that either break existing successes or add nothing.

## Complement or hinder

The midpoint tool complements the framework when used as a curvature-remainder diagnostic on measured profiles. It hinders when used to justify a pre-chosen curved shape that sacrifices light bending or the Newtonian limit. Catenary and Gamma, in the forms proposed, hinder. Disciplined use: keep the linear cone, apply the remainder test to the data, and only consider a different profile if the remainder is reproducibly non-zero.
