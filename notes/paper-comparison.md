# Paper appendix comparison

Date: 9 October 2026

Checked the appendices in kawastony/Quantum_Gravity, mainly paper _9_from_geometry.docx, paper_13_cone_geometry.docx, paper_25_consolidated.pdf, and Paper_26_Consolidated.pdf.

## Already in the paper appendices

- Cone metric. Paper 9, Appendix A.1: ds^2 = dr^2 + beta(r)^2 r^2 d theta^2, and a geodesic acceleration that becomes v_inf = (Lambda_*^2 G M mu)^(1/4) for r >> r_p. Paper 25, section 2.1: ds^2 = dr^2 + beta^2 r^2 d phi^2 + dz^2, deficit delta = 2 pi (1 - beta).
- Two boundaries of a sector. Paper 25, section 3: parent field on [psi_1, psi_2], projected to the endpoints. Not the x^2 midpoint identity.
- A pause scale. Paper 25, section 2.2 and Appendix C: r_p = 1/mu, crossover between compressive and tensile regimes. Paper 26, section 2.2: inner active regime, outer tensile regime. A radial scale, not an elongation clock.
- An integral over the cone. Paper 9, Appendix A.3: dQ_top/dt = - (1/2) c_twist^2 H(t) Omega_DM, integrated from recombination to today, cone angle freezing near z ~ 0.35.
- A quadratic. Paper 9, Appendix A.4: second derivative of a cosine potential in the cone angle, giving m_phi = 18.7 eV. Paper 25: quadratic mediator potential. Not the statement that the derivative at x = 3 equals 2 + 4.
- Fixed angular period. Paper 26, section 2.1: ds^2_int = d rho^2 + a(rho)^2 d psi^2 + sum b_i(rho)^2 d theta_i^2, with theta_i of period 2 pi. A widening internal profile, not a fixed enclosed volume under stretch.

## Not in the paper appendices

- The x^2 midpoint identity, and symmetric collapse from (-1, 7) to (2, 4) with the midpoint reading held at 6.
- Volume exact and circumference fixed while the funnel elongates without bound.
- Closing speed of the boundary points as the acceleration of water in the cone.
- Active/pause rule that the water always reaches the tip in the same time.
- Integral ledger: area 12w under a constant slope, impulse (L' - L)/T between pauses, kinetic energy growing like L^2.

The shared words are cone, boundary, pause, integral, and acceleration. The constructions are not the same.
