# Intended reading

Date: 9 October 2026

The boundaries 2 and 4, and -1 and 7, were a concept, not the exact numbers of the tool.

What the tool was meant to explain:

A person is standing. A deep voice speaks, and it feels like falling, faster and faster. The speed of the fall is increasing. A soft voice speaks, and the falling stops. It feels safe. After an equal interval the deep voice speaks again. The fall continues at the speed it had already reached, and that speed increases further. The soft voice returns, the pause returns, and then the fall resumes again.

An independent observer advises him in a way that does not affect the dynamics. The advice is to accept his fate. As soon as he accepts it, he comes out of the trap. The intended likeness is the equivalence principle.

Reading kept next to that account:

- The deep voice is the active phase. Speed increases.
- The soft voice is the pause. "The falling stops" cannot mean the speed is reset to zero, because the next deep voice continues from the speed already reached. The pause holds the speed. What stops is the increase.
- Equal intervals mean the active phase and the pause have the same duration.
- The observer does not couple. The advice changes whether he is fighting the motion, not the motion.
- Acceptance is the exit condition. In the equivalence principle, free fall is locally indistinguishable from no force. The felt trap is the resistance to the fall, not the fall itself. That is an analogy, not a derivation of the principle from the midpoint identity.
