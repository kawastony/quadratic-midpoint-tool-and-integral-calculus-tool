# Motivated carrier

Date: 9 October 2026

Three attempts on the effective law. Still not a theorem of the packet Hamiltonian.

## A. Why the active half

The full-cycle integral of a(t) is zero. A carrier that accepted both halves would store nothing. The selection is the dissipative split: positive work leaves the packet and enters the cone; negative work stays in the packet as the return of its own centroid. The cone elongation is fed only while the boundaries are closing. When the constitution holds them, the coupling is off. That is a rectifier, motivated by one-way feed into the funnel, not by the two-level algebra.

## B. Average law

Rectify, then average over the clock T = pi / Delta E.

g_eff = (1/T) integral_{a>0} a(t) dt = Delta v / T.

At the locked point Delta v = 0.07084 and T = 44.35, so g_eff = 0.00160 in packet units. This is the coarse-grained source. It is not the raw average, which is zero. The rectifier is the step that makes it nonzero.

## C. Receiver from the carrier

The stored impulse defines a potential on the generator by dPhi/ds = -F(s)/m, with F the spread force. The test body has action integral of (1/2 m sdot^2 - m Phi) dt. Its equation is

d^2 s / dt^2 = - dPhi/ds = g(s).

It does not inherit the voice schedule. The pause is on the source. Once the impulse is stored, Phi is steady between cycles, and the test body accelerates continuously with g(s). Copying the deep and soft rules onto the receiver was the stronger, unnecessary claim. This replaces it.

## Status

Active-half selection is motivated by one-way feed and dissipation. The average law is the rectified clock average. The receiver follows the carrier potential alone. None of the three is forced by the oscillatory Hamiltonian. Together they are a motivated effective model rather than a copied rulebook.
